## What it is

Saying no is refusing work while keeping the relationship, the record and the option of revisiting. Done well it is a decision with a reason and a condition for reversal, delivered to the person who asked.

## Why it matters

Every team has more requests than capacity, so the ordering is made of refusals whether or not anyone says them out loud. Unspoken no's arrive as items that never get picked up, which the requester reads as neglect. A stated no with a reason is cheaper for everyone: the requester can go elsewhere, escalate knowingly, or come back with better evidence.

## Core ideas

### Say no to the item, not the person

Acknowledge the need, then refuse the specific solution. The need is usually real even when the proposal is wrong.

```text
"That is a real problem — I see it in the tickets too.
 I am not going to build a second dashboard for it this quarter,
 because the same data is missing from the first one.
 If the fix is the data, it is a two-week job and I would move it up."
```

Note the reframe from a dashboard to the underlying fix.

### Give the reason, the trade and the condition

Three parts: what it loses to, what it would cost, and what would change your mind. A no without a condition is a wall; with one, it is a decision the requester can influence.

### Route the request instead of dropping it

There may be a better owner: support, a configuration change, an existing feature nobody found, or the next quarter. Route it and say so.

### Keep a visible refusal log

The same request arriving monthly is a signal. Log what was refused, when and why; the second occurrence is evidence about the request and the third is evidence about the process.

### Reserve escalation as a legitimate path

People have the right to escalate; the role is to make escalation informed rather than necessary. Publish the order and the reasoning and anyone can see where their item sits.

## Practice

### Refuse one request properly

Take the most recently deferred request and write the answer: the acknowledged need, the item refused, what it loses to, and the condition that would change the decision.

Done when: the requester can state the condition under which it comes back, and has been told who else might help.

### Start a refusal log

Record the last ten requests you did not take, with the date and the reason, and look for repeats.

Done when: at least one repeat shows a request that is better answered by a route other than the backlog.

## Common mistakes

- **Silence instead of a no** — the requester reads neglect and asks again; fix: answer with a decision and a reason.
- **Refusing the person rather than the proposal** — the relationship is spent for nothing; fix: acknowledge the need and refuse the specific solution.
- **No condition for reversal** — the refusal cannot be challenged or influenced; fix: state what evidence would bring it back.
- **Dropping the request without routing** — a problem that someone else could solve stays unsolved; fix: name another owner or an existing feature.
- **No record of refusals** — the same request recurs and every conversation starts from scratch; fix: keep a dated refusal log.
