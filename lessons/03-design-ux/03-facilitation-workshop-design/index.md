## What it is

Facilitation is running a structured session that gets a group to a decision. Workshop design is the preparation that makes it possible: choosing the decision, the people, the sequence of activities and the artefacts everyone will walk out holding. The output is a decision and its evidence — not a wall of sticky notes photographed for the archive.

## Why it matters

Groups of smart people routinely fail to decide anything in two hours, then repeat the meeting next week. The cause is almost never the content; it is an agenda that runs out of time before the question is answered, one voice filling every silence, and no record of who agreed to what. A designed session compresses weeks of scattered conversation into a morning, and the record it produces settles later arguments about what was decided. It is also the cheapest way to give a mixed group the shared evidence that design thinking and lean cycles depend on.

## Core ideas

### Design backwards from the decision

Write the decision at the top of the page, in one sentence, with the date it is needed. Everything that does not serve it is cut. If you cannot write the decision, the session is not yet designed.

```text
Decision: choose one onboarding flow to build this quarter.
Owner: Maya. Needed by: Thursday. Participants: 6. Room: 90 minutes.
```

### A timed agenda with an activity per block

Every block names its activity, its output and its owner. Times are ceilings. Prompts are written out in advance, because the wording of a prompt decides the quality of the answers.

```text
Workshop: onboarding decision. Total 90 minutes.
00-05  Frame      Read the decision statement aloud; no discussion.
05-20  Evidence   Walk the wall of five research quotes; silent reading, then dots.
20-45  Diverge    4 groups x 2 sketches; prompt: "one screen, no words you would not say aloud"
45-70  Converge   Silent dot vote, then 1 minute per person to argue for one dot.
70-85  Decide     Choose, name the owner, name the first build. Read it back.
85-90  Close      Confirm next check-in; photograph only the decision board.
```

### The facilitator holds no opinion

A facilitator who argues for their own option stops being able to manage the room. Take the content hat off; if you must contribute, hand the pen to someone else for that block. The same applies to the person with the most authority: their opinion stated first collapses the divergence.

### Capture one artefact per block

Each block produces something visible that the next block consumes: quotes feed dot votes, dot votes feed groups, groups feed the decision board. Note that a "parking lot" sheet for off-topic items protects the agenda without dismissing the contribution.

### Close in the room

Read the decision, the owner and the date aloud before anyone leaves. Send the one-page record within the day. A decision that is not written down re-opens within a week.

## Practice

### Design a 90-minute decision workshop

Write your decision statement, pick no more than eight people, and fill in a timed agenda with one activity and one output per block, prompts included. Send it to one participant in advance for a sense check.

Done when: an invited participant who has not spoken to you can say what they will have contributed and what will be decided.

### Run it and write the record

Facilitate the session you designed, then send a one-page record the same day: the decision, the evidence it rested on, the owner, the date, and the parking lot.

Done when: a person who was not in the room can act on the record without asking a question.

## Common mistakes

- **No stated decision** — the session becomes a discussion that runs until time is up; fix: write the decision and its owner at the top of the agenda.
- **An agenda of topics** — "onboarding" is not an activity and will consume whatever time it is given; fix: name the activity, the output and its duration for every block.
- **The senior person speaking first** — everyone else's divergence collapses into agreement; fix: gather written or sketched input before opening the discussion.
- **The facilitator arguing their own case** — they lose the room's trust and stop steering; fix: hand the pen over or stay silent for that block.
- **No record the same day** — the decision re-opens and the session is repeated; fix: send the one-page record before the end of the day.
