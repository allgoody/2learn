## What it is

Miro is an infinite whiteboard for group work: workshops, journey maps, affinity diagrams and anything else that needs a wall rather than a page. It is where a remote or hybrid group does the sticky-note part of design together, with the notes, the votes and the parking lot in one place.

## Why it matters

The physical wall is still the best collaborative tool ever made, and it stops working the moment the group is not in one room. A shared board restores most of what the wall gave: everyone can see the whole picture at once, move things around, and vote anonymously. It also leaves a record, which the wall and the photographs of the wall never did.

## Core ideas

### Reach for it when the work is many small items

A board is good at dozens of things that need moving, grouping and voting. It is a poor place to write a document or hold a decision table; those belong in a wiki or a document, linked from the board.

### Set the board up before the session

Pre-place the frames, prompts and dot votes. An empty board costs the group ten minutes of orientation and loses the first idea while everyone finds the toolbar.

```text
Frame 1  Prompt and the decision statement
Frame 2  Evidence wall (five quotes, added)
Frame 3  Six sketch areas, one per group
Frame 4  Dot voting grid
Frame 5  Parking lot
```

### Use anonymous voting

Visible voting produces a bandwagon. Anonymous dot voting and a reveal gives the quiet half of the room the same weight as the loud half, which is the main reason to run a vote at all.

### Give every item an owner and an action

A board of ideas is not a decision. Every chosen item gets a name and a date, on the board, before the session ends.

### Close the board

Archive it read-only and copy the decisions into the place the team works daily. A board left open becomes a second, staler source of truth.

## Practice

### Build a workshop board from an agenda

Take the timed agenda from Facilitation & Workshop Design and turn each block into a prepared frame with its prompt and its voting area.

Done when: the board can be opened by a participant with no explanation and they know where to type in each block.

### Run an affinity sort on the board

Take twenty observations from research, scatter them, and have three people group them silently, then name each group together.

Done when: the groups are named in the participants' words and the merged list is three to five findings.

## Common mistakes

- **An empty board at the start** — the group spends the session's first ten minutes orienting; fix: pre-place frames and prompts.
- **Visible voting** — the first votes pull the rest; fix: vote anonymously and reveal together.
- **Boards with no decisions** — a wall of notes with no owner; fix: attach a name and a date to every chosen item before the session ends.
- **Using it as a document store** — the board becomes an unsearchable second source of truth; fix: copy decisions into the team's document tool and archive the board.
- **Leaving the board open** — people keep editing after the decision, and the record becomes ambiguous; fix: close and archive it when the session ends.
