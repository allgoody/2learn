## Example 1 — Turning a rescue into a finding

A participant cannot find the control that sends a saved draft. The lines below come from a session where the guide was skimmed and every pause was filled by the moderator. Rewrite the exchange so the participant works it out, and keep the guide's timings.

```text
Guide as run: no timings, prompts improvised.
08-35  Task: "find the send button"
Moderator: "it's in the footer, bottom right — see it?"
Participant: "ah yes, got it." [clicks]
Moderator: "and how did that feel?"
```

### Solution

```text
Guide as written, kept in front of the moderator:
00-03  Warm-up: "Tell me about the last time you sent a draft."
08-35  Task: three tasks on cards, read aloud, no hints.
       Card: "Send your saved draft to the customer."
35-45  Debrief: "What stood out? What did you expect to happen?"
45-50  Close: thank, incentive, anything to add.

Exchange as run:
[participant reads the card, scans the header, scrolls down, stops]
Moderator: [counts to five, says nothing]
Participant: "I would expect send up here... the menu? No. Maybe at the
bottom, where the last button was."
[scrolls to the footer, finds it]
Participant: "there it is. I did not look down there."
```

The second version keeps the failure, and the hesitation is now the finding: the send action was looked for in the header and the menu first, which is worth a note. The scripted guide is what lets the moderator look up rather than improvise, and the five-second count is what replaces the rescue. The usual mistake is answering the "where is it?" question directly — the session becomes a demo, and the participant's difficulty disappears from the notes along with the evidence.
