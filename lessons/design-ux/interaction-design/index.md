## What it is

Interaction design shapes moment-to-moment behaviour: what happens when someone taps, what the interface says while it works, what it shows when it fails, and how one step leads to the next. It is the discipline of states and feedback, not of how things look — that is visual design, and the two are decided separately even when they are drawn together.

## Why it matters

Most complaints about an interface are not about its appearance but about its behaviour: a button that gave no sign it was pressed, a form that lost the input, a state that never seemed to end. These are invisible in a static layout and surface only when someone uses the thing. Interaction design decides them deliberately rather than by default, and the cost of getting them wrong is trust — the user presses twice, then stops trusting the interface entirely.
