## What it is

User research is finding out what people actually need and do before designing anything for them. It covers choosing a method that fits the question, getting the right people into the room, running the session without leading them, and turning what you saw into a decision the team will act on.

## Why it matters

Every product decision is a guess about human behaviour; research decides how expensive that guess is. A team that has watched ten people attempt the task argues differently — about what to cut, not about whose intuition is better. It also protects against the most common failure of experienced designers, which is designing for themselves. The cost is real: recruiting takes a week, and a badly run session produces confident nonsense. That is why the four stages below are learned in order.

## In this track

1. **Choosing a method** (`design-ux/user-research/01-choosing-a-method`) — matching the question you are asking to a method that can answer it.
2. **Recruiting participants** (`design-ux/user-research/02-recruiting-participants`) — finding people who actually do the thing, without selecting for the ones who will be kind.
3. **Running a session** (`design-ux/user-research/03-running-a-session`) — the plan, the prompts and the discipline that keep the evidence clean.
4. **From findings to decisions** (`design-ux/user-research/04-from-findings-to-decisions`) — turning notes into findings, and findings into a change someone owns.
