## What it is

Accessibility is the work of making a page usable by people who navigate with a keyboard, a screen reader, a magnifier or a voice, and by anyone whose situation temporarily resembles theirs — an arm in a sling, a bright screen, a noisy train. WCAG is the published standard those guarantees are measured against. "a11y" is the same word with its middle letters replaced by their count.

## Why it matters

The web is how people apply for work, pay tax and see a doctor, so an inaccessible page is not a small defect — it excludes people from things they have no other route to. It is also enforceable: accessibility is a legal requirement in the public sector across most of Europe and in a growing number of countries for private services. Practically, the biggest wins come from the cheapest habits, which is why this track sits next to HTML and CSS rather than in a separate discipline. You cannot bolt accessibility on at the end; you write it as you go or you rewrite.
